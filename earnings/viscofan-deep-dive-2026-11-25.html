The business in one paragraph

Viscofan is the world's largest maker of artificial casings for meat products (cellulose, collagen, fibrous, plastic), with 22 plants in 21 countries and 5,721 people at the end of 2025. Casings are a small cost for its customers, so it can pass on price, and the business is defensive: volumes follow processed-meat consumption, not the economic cycle. Its weak spots are input costs (collagen hides, viscose chemicals, energy in Spain, where it also runs co-generation) and currencies: it sells in USD and EUR but produces in Mexico, Brazil and Czechia.

Bull case

  • Global leader in a niche oligopoly; casings are a tiny share of customers' costs, and price increases stick (May–June 2026 rounds held gross margin at 65.5% in 3Q).
  • Like-for-like growth is real: +4.5% revenue and +1.6% EBITDA in 9M26 despite energy and hide inflation; record quarterly revenue in 3Q.
  • Currencies turn from headwind to tailwind (−6.2 pp on EBITDA in 9M26); the stronger USD already helped in 3Q.
  • Capacity projects in New Jersey and Zacapu are complete; the Czech plant comes in 2028. Health and Pet Treats are small but growing fast.
  • Low leverage (≈0.9× net bank debt / EBITDA) and a shareholder-friendly policy; a defensive profile scores well in recession and stagflation regimes.

Bear case

  • Free cash flow (€94.8m in 2025) does not cover the payout (€145.1m), so debt funds the gap; capex rises to an average of €120m.
  • EBITDA margin is sliding (23.7% → 23.2% → 21.5% in 9M26) while the plan needs 25% by 2030.
  • Guidance cut within eight months of the plan launch; Q4 still needs a strong energy and currency outcome.
  • Producing in Mexico and Brazil means stronger local currencies raise costs even when the USD helps revenue.
  • Working capital keeps absorbing cash (inventory to sales cut to 30.9% in 2025, but inventories +13.6% in 9M26).
